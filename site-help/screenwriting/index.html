Formatting scripts and screenplays

This page gives a clear, readable house style for scripts and screenplays published on Strange Country. It is intended for web publication rather than formal submission to a studio or production company.

Scene headings

Use a second-level heading for each new scene:

## INT. HOTEL CORRIDOR — NIGHT

Keep the usual screenplay order: interior or exterior, place, then time of day.

Action

Write action in ordinary paragraphs and in the present tense:

Mara stops outside the last door. Music is playing somewhere below.

Begin a new paragraph when the action changes focus or a different beat begins.

Character names and dialogue

Put the character’s name in bold capitals. Add a backslash at the end of the line so the dialogue begins immediately below it:

**MARA**\
You said the room was empty.

Begin a new paragraph before the next speaker:

**ELIAS**\
It was empty when I left.

Parentheticals

Use a brief italic parenthetical only when the delivery is not clear from the dialogue:

**MARA**\
*(quietly)*\
Then somebody came back.

Do not use parentheticals to direct every line.

Transitions

Put an occasional transition in bold capitals on its own line:

**CUT TO:**

Use transitions sparingly. A new scene heading is usually enough.

Hard line breaks

A backslash at the very end of a line forces the next text onto a new line without beginning a new paragraph:

**MARA**\
You said it was finished.

Do not put a space after the backslash.

A complete example

## EXT. RAILWAY PLATFORM — DAWN

Rain moves through the station lights. Elias stands beneath the clock with a small case at his feet.

**MARA**\
You came after all.

**ELIAS**\
*(looking down the track)*\
I said I would try.

A whistle sounds beyond the bridge.

**CUT TO:**

## INT. EMPTY CARRIAGE — MORNING

The case sits alone on the seat.
