Formatting your writing

Strange Country uses Markdown, the simple formatting system built into iA Writer. Begin with the first sections for everyday writing; the later sections cover less common forms when you need them.

You can type the examples directly. For several common forms—including headings, links, lists, bold, and italics—you can also use iA Writer’s Format menu.

Everyday formatting

Paragraphs and line breaks

Leave one blank line between paragraphs:

This is the first paragraph.

This is the second paragraph.

To begin a new line without starting a new paragraph, put a backslash \ at the very end of the line:

MARA\
You said it was finished.

ELIAS\
I said it was nearly finished.

The backslash will not appear on the published page. Do not put a space after it.

Headings

Strange Country already supplies the title. Do not type it again or begin with a single #.

Use two number signs for a main section and three for a subsection:

## The Journey North

### The Last Evening in Naples

Leave a blank line before and after a heading. Use levels in order rather than jumping from ## to ####.

Italics, bold, and strikethrough

*italics*

**bold**

~~strikethrough~~

Italics suit ordinary emphasis and titles of books or films. Use bold more sparingly.

Links

The report is available from the [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/).

The words inside square brackets are what the reader sees; the address goes inside parentheses.

For an email address:

[Write to the editor](mailto:editor@example.com)

Quotations

Use ordinary quotation marks for short quotations inside a paragraph.

For a longer quotation, begin each paragraph with >:

> This is a longer quotation set apart from the main text.
>
> A second paragraph of the same quotation begins this way.

An attribution may follow in ordinary prose beneath the quotation.

Lists

Bulleted list:

- First item
- Second item
- Third item

Numbered list:

1. First step
2. Second step
3. Third step

Leave a blank line before and after the list.

Footnotes

This sentence needs a note.[^1]

[^1]: The text of the note goes here.

Use a different label for each note. The labels need not be consecutive numbers; the published page will arrange the notes.

Images and captions

Before placing an image, keep the file in the folder belonging to that Post or musing. See Add an image for the Finder instructions and filename rules.

Place an image on a line of its own, with a blank line above and below:

![An empty railway platform in winter](railway-platform.jpg)

The words inside square brackets briefly describe what the image shows. They help readers who cannot see it and appear if the image cannot be loaded. The filename inside parentheses must match the actual filename exactly.

Add a visible caption in quotation marks after the filename:

![An empty railway platform in winter](railway-platform.jpg "The station shortly after dawn")

The description and caption serve different purposes. The description says what the image shows; the caption can supply context, a date, a location, or a credit.

A complete passage might look like this:

The station was already deserted by the time we arrived.

![An empty railway platform beneath a snow-covered roof](railway-platform.jpg "The station shortly after dawn")

The last train had passed several hours earlier.

Do not add width or size instructions. Strange Country will fit the image to the page automatically.

Dividers

To mark a clear change of scene or subject:

---

Put the divider on a line of its own, with a blank line above and below it.

Further formatting

Tables

| City | Date | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Palermo | 5 August | Arrival |
| Naples | 9 August | Departure |

The second line is required. Colons control alignment: :--- left, ---: right, and :---: centred.

Tables work best for compact information. On a narrow screen, a very wide table may need horizontal scrolling.

Nested lists

Indent a subordinate item by four spaces:

- Sicily
    - Palermo
    - Catania
- Campania
    - Naples

For a paragraph within a list item, indent that paragraph to match the item.

Code and preformatted text

Use single backticks for a short command or filename:

Enter `strange-country preview` in Terminal.

For a longer block, place three backticks before and after it:

```text
This text keeps its spacing
and appears in a separate block.
```

You may replace text with a language name such as python, html, or sh when useful.

Definition lists

For a term followed by its definition:

Private
: Writing that is not on the public website.

Ready to publish
: Private writing that will be included in the next publication.

Use a blank line between separate entries.

Writing Markdown characters literally

Put a backslash before a character that Markdown would otherwise treat as formatting:

\*This appears with literal asterisks.\*

The price increased by 10\%.

Backticks are often clearer for commands, filenames, or literal punctuation.

Raw HTML and special layouts

Raw HTML inside ordinary writing is intentionally not published. This keeps the site consistent and prevents a pasted fragment from breaking the page.

For ordinary writing, use the Markdown forms above. Strange Country already supplies supported layouts for images, tables, footnotes, quotations, code, and headings.

When a piece genuinely needs a special layout that Markdown cannot express, ask the person maintaining the website to add a supported shortcode or layout. Do not paste scripts, style blocks, iframe code, or copied webpage HTML into a writing document.

A complete example

The road followed the coast for several miles before turning inland.

## The Journey North

We left shortly before sunrise. A contemporary account describes the scene:

> The harbour remained dark, save for a single lamp above the customs house.

The surviving records list three stops:

1. Palermo
2. Messina
3. Naples

![A harbour seen from the quay at dawn](harbour-at-dawn.jpg "The eastern quay, 1932")

The date in the ledger is uncertain.[^1]

[^1]: The final digit is partly obscured by water damage.
